Reading: the underlying ideas are known. Tighten-only delegated authority is XACML's policy-combining and delegation model and AWS IAM permission boundaries; deterministic forbid-wins evaluation outside the model is Cedar and OPA, and this engine's decision rule is the same forbid-wins; default-deny and explicit reasons are standard; guardrails outside an LLM-controlled robot are RoboGuard's point. What this project contributes is the combination and the packaging: two kinds of waiting as first-class outcomes (a person must approve, signed and bound; the situation must change), a ranked authority label on every rule that classifies conflicts at authoring time (the real difference from Cedar: not a different decision rule, but a label that says which of two clashing rules is the override), one rule language and one decision path for robots, people wearing glasses and AI agents, whole-plan checking on projected facts, and a language-neutral conformance suite so a port can prove it decides the same. The others either decide well but leave participant semantics to the application (Cedar), put the AI inside the loop (ASIMOV is a benchmark and a method for generating a constitution from data, evaluated by having the model apply it; RoboGuard grounds rules with an LLM and then checks plans formally), or dispatch and coordinate robot traffic (Open-RMF). Theirs are more mature. Cedar's analysis covers a richer policy language than this one; on this rule language the analysis here is exact and the [Cedar adapter](adapters/cedar/) carries the deny/permit subset over, saying what it drops. RoboGuard is the reference for plan *synthesis* — turning rules and a scene into a plan — which this project leaves to planners.

**Layers, not competitors.** In a real deployment these sit on top of each other: certified safety controllers and the standards behind them (ISO 10218, 3691-4, 13482) decide whether a motion is physically safe; traffic coordination (Open-RMF, or your own) decides who goes where when; a permission and authority layer (this, or Cedar for software agents) decides what each participant may do here, by whose say-so, and what needs a person; plan checking for AI planners (this; RoboGuard's grounding for the synthesis side) checks a proposed plan before any step runs; and AI proposing on top — rules in plain words, plans, explanations — never inside the decision.
